# Assigned record maintenance

- Maintain each attached effort's `effort.md` when validated meaning or where the work stands changes, including completion and blockers. Keep accepted design in its owner and proposals in their producer artifacts until accepted. Records describe state and leave next steps out, as [storage](storage.md#layout) describes.
- Record each established decision where it lands, once, and refer to it by ID everywhere else. A decision about the work goes in the `effort.md` of the effort that owns it, with a pointer from any other effort it affects. A decision about a designed system goes in that design: a stable design's `design.md` with a `changes.md` entry citing the session or meeting, following `design-docs`, or the effort's local `design.md`. Touch `effort.md` for it only when where the work stands changed. The attached efforts come from the compaction note or `catalog_find` sessions.
- Edit records in place so they hold what is still live. Preserve consequential rationale and corrections, directly or through a relevant synthesis pointer, when removing superseded detail. Conversation exports retain history; they should not be the only way to recover reasoning still needed for continuation.
- Cairn generates each effort's index from the catalog. Make completed material findable by describing it, not by listing it. Record corrections and replacements with `supersedes` on `catalog_describe`, and relationships with `informs`; don't edit other producers' accounts. Read new concise learnings and pass them to the tasks they help.
- Use pointers instead of repeating research findings. What is known about an existing subject belongs in its [knowledge article](knowledge.md), not in the effort's records. Supply selected relevant material to successors with applicability and authority status. Background files never replace the assignment.
- Keep one writer per shared file: each brief names the paths its writer owns, and the catalog's session tree records the writers. Producers own their evidence. Finish writes before releasing readers and serialize subsequent updates with dependent reads.
- Human-facing orchestration uses Scribe where its harness supports the required synchronization. Delegated orchestrators maintain their own workstream records directly. A bounded capture can be written directly and does not create a standing Scribe.
- Before assigning synchronization or handling compaction, read the applicable [OpenCode](continuity/opencode.md) or [Claude Code](continuity/claude-code.md) reference. Those mechanisms implement maintenance; they do not expand its scope.
- Preserve exact blocked-write conflicts and attributed content for a permitted fallback owner. Never bypass a tool denial.
- Routine record updates need no separate user-facing announcement. Stop maintaining when the assignment ends or the human exits the workflow; finish or explicitly transfer pending writes and preserve remaining work.
