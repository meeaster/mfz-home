# Design docs

**Starting or changing what a design says?** Prepare or refresh its working evidence packet with [playbooks/prepare.md](playbooks/prepare.md) before the task below. Keep it current while discussing and applying changes; delegated gathering covers relevant cross-design impacts so the author need not reload the source pile. Wording-only and local presentation fixes stay on the editing path.

**Researching a design's open questions?** Follow [playbooks/research.md](playbooks/research.md): it finds what the knowledge base already knows, researches the rest, and records the evidence and answers directly.

**Recommending what to decide?** Follow [playbooks/recommend.md](playbooks/recommend.md): it writes or refreshes the AI recommendation on open decisions and questions.

**Tightening what a design already says** (shorter, said once, references named in words)? Follow [playbooks/edit.md](playbooks/edit.md): it drafts the rewrites locally, shows them side by side in a review copy of the doc, and applies the ones the user wants.

**Updating an existing design from a meeting, an email or answers?** Follow [playbooks/update.md](playbooks/update.md): first propose what it could change and let the user settle the proposals, then apply them. The playbook holds everything a routine update needs, so you don't need the rest of this page or the references unless it sends you there. Everything else (starting a design, making or restructuring pages, drawing diagrams, publishing) starts below.

A design is the lasting, agreed record of a body of work: what is being built or changed, why it's worth doing, how it works, its plan, and what has been decided. It sits in its own folder while the efforts on it come and go, and it is what others review and approve. Its `design.json` holds the problem, goals and how it works in prose, and the working direction when one is set, then facts, requirements, the plan, decisions and their options, parts, risks and open questions, and the Confluence pages, Jira items and other links around it; `evidence.json` and `meetings.json` beside it hold the evidence and the meetings. Together they are the single source of truth. `design.json` is read in full by whoever picks the design up next, so its prose carries the connected understanding and its records carry the facts; relevant evidence and meetings are covered by preparation, with the author's reads described in [What to read](#what-to-read). The doc is how the design is shown to people who don't all know the system: pages of prose and pictures, with every table, option card and decision map rendered from the records and every reference opening a card. Pages are short Markdown files; the skill renders their structure, and what's particular to a design (its pictures, and any part better built for it than reused) is a component in its folder, bound to the records so it follows their status. It records where the design stands, never what to do next; an agent proposes that from the records when someone asks. The published doc is one HTML file that goes anywhere.

## What to read

Accuracy means complete relevant consideration across the work, not every agent rereading every source. [Preparation](playbooks/prepare.md) covers evidence and cross-design dependencies; the author reads the current packet and the artifact it changes. Broaden gathering when relevance is uncertain, rather than assuming a local edit has local consequences.

| Task | Author or reviewer reads | Skill references |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Start a design, change direction, or rewrite a version | Current packet; `design.json` in full when present; affected records/pages/components and changes since handoff | records, lifecycle, writing, pages; components, diagrams and security for what is authored |
| Make or change a decision, compare options, change the plan | Current packet and `design.json` in full; affected records/pages/components | records, lifecycle, writing; components and diagrams if a picture changes |
| Take in a meeting, email or research answer | Current packet, `design.json` in full, changes since handoff, and source/record passages needed to apply the outcome | task playbook; other references only where it sends you |
| Wording-only or local presentation edit | Editing playbook's reads; affected content and its references | edit; components or diagrams for changed markup |
| Sign-off, publishing or comprehensive review | Whole current design, partitioned among reviewers when large, checked against packet and relevant original support | records, lifecycle (review) |

- Supported packet claims need no automatic original-source reread. Reopen for contradiction, missing consequential detail, ambiguous authority, material freshness or a claim beyond coverage; refresh broad dependencies through gathering.
- Every change ends with the sweep in [lifecycle](references/lifecycle.md#working-on-it): every place in the folder that states an ID or fact you changed agrees with it. A packet never substitutes for checking the resulting design.

## Start a design

1. **Pin down what's needed.** Is it one question to settle in a meeting (a brief), a picture of what's being built (an overview), or more detail on one part of an existing design (an area page)? A new design starts with an overview and no areas; split one off when a part has requirements, decisions and questions of its own. Check whether a design for this system already exists; extend it rather than starting another. Establish the audience and any meeting it's for, and whether the design is open or has a working direction someone has set ([writing](references/writing.md#working-direction)). Ask when that's unclear; otherwise proceed and state assumptions. See [pages](references/pages.md).
2. **Get the folder** as described in [lifecycle](references/lifecycle.md), copy the template into it, and link the design to the current effort.
3. **Reconcile the working packet** with the request and sources through [preparation](playbooks/prepare.md). Use its supported findings and known constraints; gather beyond its scope when needed. Anything unknown becomes an open question, not a guess; name who to ask only when someone said who.
4. **Write design.json and evidence.json**, per [records](references/records.md): the problem, goals and how it works in prose, then facts, requirements, the plan's deliverables, decisions with their options, parts, risks and questions in design.json, and the evidence in evidence.json, each record with `Recorded from` and each open decision and question with an `Explanation` of what it asks and where it fits. Mark how each open question can be answered (`Answer from`: research an agent could do, a person, or an approval). Judge each requirement three ways (today, this design, still to show) and give each open question when its answer is needed. Check that every claim and every part has a finding or a question behind it. Any number or figure stated in more than one place (a count, a cost, a date) becomes a fact under `## Facts` and is written `{fact:key}` everywhere else.
5. **Write the pages** from the templates, following [writing](references/writing.md), [components](references/components.md), and [diagrams](references/diagrams.md): the prose in each page's Markdown, and for everything else whatever shows it best: a skill component where one fits as it is, or a component of the design's own (every picture, and any section built for this problem), with the parts that wait on a decision bound to it. The skill's components are for what repeats, not a shape to force a design into; when one is close but not right, see [components](references/components.md#reuse-or-build). Draw "What we're after" with the real products and platforms named, and show a working direction under the overview's `## Working direction`. Add sections a page needs and drop ones with nothing real to say.
6. **Add security views** when the design changes where data lives or travels, or the environment is regulated, per [security](references/security.md). Put compliance obligations in Requirements.
7. **Log the change** in changes.md, then **check and build** with `scripts/doc.mjs`. Fix all errors and read the warnings.
8. **Look at the built doc** in a browser, or with `scripts/shot.mjs` when no browser tool is available. Visit every page you changed. Fix clipped text, crossing labels, and misaligned arrows. Click a few markers to see their cards, including ones whose item lives on another page, and switch to the security view.
9. **Review what it says**, per [lifecycle](references/lifecycle.md#reviewing-what-it-says): `check` proves structure, not truth. Read the prose and your own diagrams against the records for existing versus proposed, confirmed versus unconfirmed, open versus chosen, intended versus demonstrated controls, and any fact that reads differently on two pages. Fix and rebuild.
10. **Report back** with the design folder and built doc, the pages, the decisions and their status, the open questions and when each is needed, what the review covered and changed, and anything unverified.

## Continue a design

- **Pick it up** the way [lifecycle](references/lifecycle.md) describes: prepare or refresh the packet for substantive work, read design.json in full and changes.md since the last handoff, take the task's reads from [What to read](#what-to-read), and run `check` to see whether the published doc is behind. When it says the folder is in an older format, run `migrate` and review the result first.
- **Record findings as you establish them.** Change a decision's status, the requirements, or a deliverable, or defer a question, only on the user's word or a meeting's accepted outcome, and log each accepted change in changes.md. Record each fact once, where it lands, and refer to it by ID everywhere else, following [lifecycle](references/lifecycle.md#designs-and-efforts).
- **Keep it consistent as you go.** Every change ends with a sweep for everywhere the same fact appears (pages, your components, other records, the effort), per [lifecycle](references/lifecycle.md#working-on-it). Fix any disagreement you find along the way, including ones that were there before you.
- **After a meeting or an email** that bears on the design, go through its candidates with the user, apply what they accept, record the meeting in meetings.json with an Outcomes line for each thing it settled and the record it changed, and log the changes, following the meeting and email steps in [lifecycle](references/lifecycle.md).
- **When a question is answered**, the answer becomes evidence and the question gets Answer and Answered by; update what it blocked, per [writing](references/writing.md#open-questions).
- **When a decision is made**, set its status in design.json with a Why (what tipped it, citing evidence), mark its options Chosen or Not chosen with why, and add Reasoning or Also considered where they help a later reader. Then bring the pages in line: bound parts and rendered cards follow on their own; redraw a picture only where what was chosen differs from what it shows, and make "In short" say what changed.
- **Work within the working direction** when the design has one: problems with it become questions or risks, and alternatives to it a brief the user asks for ([writing](references/writing.md#working-direction)). When the direction changes so far that the design would be rewritten, start a new version ([lifecycle](references/lifecycle.md#a-new-version)).
- Keep every E, Q, D, and R number stable within a version. Rebuild, re-check and review after every update, and refresh the "Updated" date on each page that changed.

## Boundaries

- Don't claim FIPS validation, an authorization, encryption, or privacy without evidence. Unconfirmed security claims are questions.
- Only mark a decision Decided when the team decided it. Use "Leaning" for an option the team favours that isn't settled. Record the reason given; don't invent alternatives that nobody weighed.
- Keep local paths, machine names, and private links in `Recorded from`, the one field that is never published. `check` blocks the obvious cases, but judgement covers the rest.
- Gather evidence read-only. Changing infrastructure or accounts is outside this skill.
- Publish, commit, or push only when the user asks, and only the built files.
