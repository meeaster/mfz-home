Log ingestion in our cloud

How firewall and customer logs are collected, filtered and kept in GovCloud: the whole system on one page, and the decisions it still depends on.

01

In short

We're putting a log pipeline between our systems and Datadog. A small group of servers running Observability Pipelines Workers (OPW) receives firewall logs from our 14 office and data-center sites and logs that customers send us. The workers drop the noise we pay for today, send the rest to Datadog, and keep a full copy in S3.

The workers run in their own small VPC in GovCloud, joined to the rest of our network through the Transit Gateway. Datadog gets filtered logs within a minute. The S3 archive keeps everything for a year, with each customer's logs kept apart from ours and from each other's.

Five decisions are still open. Where the workers live and how firewall logs reach them (D1, D2) have a brief. How customer data is kept apart (D6) is being worked out on the S3 archive page. What OPW drops before Datadog (D7) and how customers connect (D3) haven't started.

Terms used on this page

OPW
Observability Pipelines Workers. Datadog's log pipeline software, run on our own servers.
VPC
A private network in AWS. Each one has its own address range and rules.
Shared Tooling VPC
The existing network that hosts common tools, including the syslog server.
ASA
Cisco ASA, the firewalls at each site. They send logs using syslog.
Transit Gateway
The AWS hub that connects our VPCs and the site VPNs to each other.
PrivateLink
A private connection into one AWS service, with no routes into the rest of the network.
