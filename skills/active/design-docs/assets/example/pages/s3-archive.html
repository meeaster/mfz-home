S3 archive

Where archived logs live, how each customer's logs are kept apart from ours and from each other's, and who can read them.

01

In short

Every log the workers receive is also written to S3 and kept for a year (D5). The archive is the record we'd search during an investigation, and the copy we'd hand a customer who asks for their own logs.

Our own logs and customer logs go to separate buckets. Inside the customer bucket, each customer gets their own prefix and their own KMS key, so a role or key for one customer can't read another customer's logs.

Whether one customer bucket is enough, or each customer needs a bucket of their own, is still open (D6). It depends on how many customers we expect in the first year (Q8).

Terms used on this page

Prefix
The start of an object's name in S3, such as customer=acme/. Access rules can be written per prefix.
KMS key
An encryption key held in AWS KMS. Reading an object needs permission on its key as well as on the bucket.
Bucket policy
The access rules attached to a bucket. Its size limit caps how many per-customer rules fit.
Lifecycle rule
An S3 rule that deletes objects once they reach a set age.

03

Design

D6 open

Two buckets: one for our own logs and one for customer logs, each with its own key. Within the customer bucket, every customer has a prefix, a key and a role of their own.
