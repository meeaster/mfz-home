# Design assistance

- During exploration, recommend costly specialist consultation or a prototype and obtain authority before dispatch. Within authorized delivery, use necessary assistance when it materially improves the result and delegation is permitted.
- Give an architect goals, accepted constraints, checked evidence, priorities, and the pending decision. Request options, strongest cases, tradeoffs, a recommendation, uncertainty, and reversal conditions.
- Give an interface designer the audience, interface states, relevant context, constraints, and pending decision. Request implementable layout and interaction direction, accessibility, and verification needs under its owning guidance.
- Gather missing facts through `evidence-gathering`; send a combined relevant delta rather than raw traces. Keep consequential choices with their decision owner.
- Consider staged compatibility and direct cutover when both are responsible, grounding the choice in clients, data, interruption tolerance, recovery, observability, coordination, and cleanup.
- Preserve complete actionable recommendations and their proposal status. Put accepted overrides beside explicit attribution rather than replacing the artifact with a parent summary.
- Use an authorized prototype to answer a bounded unsettled logic, state-model, or interface question. Require its owning skill, an approved disposable location, observations, assumptions, checks, and a locator. Prototype success does not establish production readiness or authorize production delivery.
